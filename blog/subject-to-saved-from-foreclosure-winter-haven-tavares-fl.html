What “Subject-To” Means

In a subject-to purchase, ownership transfers to the buyer, but the existing mortgage stays in place, in the seller’s name. The buyer makes the payments from then on. If the loan is behind, the buyer usually brings it current as part of the deal.

What Sellers Should Know

  • The loan stays in your name. Your credit depends on the buyer making every payment, so work with a buyer you can verify.
  • Due-on-sale clause. Most mortgages let the lender call the loan due when the property is transferred. Understand that risk and how the buyer would handle it.
  • Get it in writing and reviewed. Have an attorney review the agreement before you sign.

Done right, subject-to can stop a foreclosure, protect your credit from a completed foreclosure, and let you move on when a normal sale can’t. Read our plain-English subject-to guide for more.
